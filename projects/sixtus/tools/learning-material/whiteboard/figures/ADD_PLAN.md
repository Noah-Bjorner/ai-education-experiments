## image
- Type is not a composed whiteboard figure. Use when the learner needs to see parts of a real object (volcano, cell, camera, painting, photo of a place) that geometry cannot construct and a graph cannot name. The board already prefers composed charts/diagrams over image; this is the exception. Pass a still picture plus leader-line labels / existing callouts — do not invent a `schematic` figure type until image+annotations are clearly not enough. Not for charts, process, venn, or anything drawable from a schema. Just takes an image url as parameter and can of course be anoated with arrows etc pointing out stuff.

## number_line
- Type `number_line`. Style: `single` | `double`. Standalone math type, next to coordinate_plot — do not fold into scale. Classic numeric axis: arrows both directions; pass the span (min/max or listed ticks) as params. `single`: one line; open/closed dots, interval shading, integer hops, inequality marks. `double`: two parallel number lines, aligned vertically so equivalent positions match (km vs minutes, percent vs amount, two units of a rate). Each line has its own tick labels and unit; hops and highlights may sit on one or both. Annotation targets: each line, tick, point, interval, and hop. Neighbor splits: not `scale` timeline/spectrum (events or qualitative stops, not numbers), not `coordinate_plot` (no second axis, no y = f(x)), not `tape_diagram` (a bar split into parts, not ticks on an axis), not `xy_chart` line. Classifier need true: numbers, inequalities, or two aligned numeric scales; false: a timeline, a coordinate plane, or a tape/bar split.

## tape_diagram
- Type `tape_diagram`. A bar (tape) split into labeled parts in one dimension — part–part–whole, fractions of a quantity, percent, simple equations (“3 + x = 10” as bars). Schema: one or more bars; each bar has ordered parts with value and label; optional total bracket. Parts are lengths, not categories of a pie and not cells of an area model. Annotation targets: each bar, each part, the total. Neighbor splits: not `pie_chart` (data parts of a whole, not a measured bar), not `number_line` (no numeric ticks/arrows into negatives), not `area_model` (that is 2D), not `geometry` (not a Euclidean construction), not `xy_chart` bar. Classifier need true: a quantity split or compared as labeled bar segments; false: a pie of categories, an array of unit squares, or points on a number line.

## area_model
- Type `area_model`. Style: `array` | `region`. A rectangle whose area is the math: multiplication, fraction × fraction, or binomials. `array`: m by n unit squares (or a labeled grid), optional shading of some cells — “3 rows of 4,” fraction of an array. `region`: one rectangle partitioned into labeled sub-rectangles (area model for (x+3)(x+1), ½ × ¾). Lengths on the sides; products in the regions. Annotation targets: the whole, each cell or region, each side length. Neighbor splits: not `geometry` (not a construction with angles and markings), not `table` / `heatmap` (cells are unit area, not text or an intensity grid), not `tape_diagram` (2D area, not a 1D tape), not `pie_chart`. Classifier need true: multiplication, factors, or fractions shown as area of a rectangle or array of unit squares; false: a Euclidean shape, a data table, or a 1D bar split.


## process
- Type `process`. Style: `flow` | `cycle` | `sequence` | `stack` | `swimlane` | `roadmap`. Use when the learner needs to see how something proceeds: a procedure, a repeating cycle, an interaction in order, layers in order, which actor owns which step, or the plan of the work with a current place marked. Shared content is labeled nodes with stable ids and directed edges with optional labels; `flow` is a DAG with a start, optional decision nodes (diamonds) for branches, layout top-to-bottom or left-to-right; `cycle` uses the same nodes/edges in a loop with no required start/end; `sequence` is exclusive fields (like distribution bins vs curves): participants plus ordered messages on lifelines, not a free graph; `stack` is ordered layers (OSI, strata, Maslow) with edges optional — a pyramid frame is a stack layout, not a type; `swimlane` is a flow whose nodes are assigned to named lanes (departments, roles) so “who does this step” is visible; `roadmap` is ordered stages of a plan or method (not a mechanism and not a history), one path, optional `current` stage so later stages read as upcoming. Annotation targets: each node, edge, lane, and (for sequence) each participant and message; for `roadmap`, each stage and the current mark. Neighbor splits: not `relationship` (no single procedure), not `scale` timeline (events in the past, not a plan), not `flow` when the lesson is “where we are in the work” rather than branching decisions, not funnel or sankey (amounts moving — those are charts). Classifier need true: a procedure, cycle, ordered stages, actors in a process, or a plan with a current step; false: entities that merely connect, set overlap, or dated events. Do not allow freeform spaghetti; cap node count; branches are decision nodes on `flow`, not a separate type. `sequence` is messages over time; `swimlane` is steps owned by lanes; `roadmap` is a plan with a you-are-here mark — do not collapse them.

## relationship
- Type `relationship`. Style: `tree` | `mindmap` | `network` | `bracket` | `onion`. Use when the learner needs to see how entities connect with no single start-to-end procedure. Shared content is labeled nodes and edges (optional edge labels); layout is the style. `tree`: at most one parent, ranked hierarchy (taxonomy, org). `mindmap`: one root, radial, looser. `network`: general web, labeled links, no required root (concept maps are this style with required edge labels). `bracket`: binary elimination tree, sports-bracket layout. `onion`: concentric nested bands, inner contained in outer, no branching and no overlap (self → family → nation, core–mantle–crust); exclusive fields: ordered rings with labels, no edges. Annotation targets: each node, edge, and (for onion) each ring. Neighbor splits: not `process` (that has a procedure or cycle), not `venn` (intersecting sets, not nested containment), not `stack` (column of layers is a process), not treemap (nested size is a chart). Classifier need true: entities and how they connect (hierarchy, web, nested containment, or map of ideas); false: a process with steps, overlapping sets, or events in time. Constrain node/edge count so layout stays drawable; do not invent a separate type for “branches,” “node splits,” or fishbone — fishbone is a `network` layout if needed later.

## venn
- Type `venn`. Two or three circles; labels for each circle and every overlap region (2-circle: A, B, AB; 3-circle: also AC, BC, ABC). Blue and orange for two sets; add red as the third. Use when the lesson is shared vs unique membership, not boxes and arrows. Schema: set count, set labels, optional label per region (empty region label allowed when that overlap is unused). Annotation targets: each circle and each region. Neighbor splits: not `relationship` (no edges), not `onion` (nested containment with no intersection), not pie (parts of one whole, not overlapping sets), not geometry (this is set regions, not a construction). Classifier need true: overlapping categories or sets; false: a hierarchy, nested rings, a process, or a single whole split into slices. Lock 2–3 circle Venn; do not build general Euler diagrams in v1.

## scale
- Type `scale`. Style: `timeline` | `spectrum`. Use when items sit on one ordered line that is not a math number line. Shared content: an axis with ordered stops; each stop has id, label, and position. `timeline`: events along time (dates or sequence in time); position is temporal, spacing need not be a uniform numeric scale. `spectrum`: qualitative continuum with endpoint labels and named stops (agree—disagree, left—right). Annotation targets: the axis, each stop, optional endpoint labels. Neighbor splits: not `number_line` (numeric math axis with ticks, inequalities, open/closed dots — already a math type above), not `xy_chart` line (measured y-values over time), not `process` (steps and branches, not marks on a line). Classifier need true: events in time or positions on a qualitative range; false: a number line, a line chart of quantities, or a procedure. Do not add `number_line` as a style here.

## table
- Type `table`. Style: `grid` | `compare` | `heatmap`. Implement all three with the type; do not defer heatmap. `grid` is a row/column header grid of cell text: 2×2 labeled quadrants (Eisenhower, political compass as four named cells), T-charts, Frayer-style four-squares. `compare` is the before → after (also then/now, misconception/correct, pros/cons): exactly two sides, each with a heading and bullet points, separately colored, a vertical divider between them — not a full grid and not two stacked figures. `heatmap` is the same row/column header grid, but each cell is a number whose fill intensity (and readable numeral) encodes magnitude — confusion matrix, correlation grid, “how often does A co-occur with B.” Shared scale across the figure; do not invent a diverging palette beyond the board theme (one ink, varying opacity or a single accent). Annotation targets: the table, each header, each cell; for `compare`, each side, its heading, and each bullet. Neighbor splits: not `xy_chart` / `coordinate_plot` (scatter or points in quadrants belong there), not `distribution` (a heatmap is two categories, not a spread of one variable), not `venn` (overlap of sets, not two lists), not `text`, not two separate figures for a simple two-sided contrast. Classifier need true: a comparison grid, a two-sided contrast, labeled 2×2 cells, or a grid of magnitudes; false: plotted points, a single list, or set overlap.

## composition
- Type `composition`. Style: `funnel` | `treemap` | `sankey`. Chart family for nested, staged, or transferring parts of a whole that pie cannot carry. Exclusive style fields, like distribution. `funnel`: ordered stages, each with a value or percentage; sections shrink down the page; color per section; percentage (or value) centered in each section. `treemap`: nested rectangles, area proportional to value, parent/child groups, labels in cells. `sankey`: amounts moving between labeled nodes (budget from departments to programs, energy through a system); nodes plus weighted links; later than funnel if layout is hard. Optional later: waffle / icon array (N of M units) if pie is a bad fit — same family, not parliament (parliament is a civic hemicycle of seats). Annotation targets: each stage, rectangle, node, or link. Neighbor splits: not `pie_chart` (one flat whole), not `process` (funnel/sankey are amounts, not branching steps), not `relationship` tree (treemap is nested size, not structure alone), not parliament. Classifier need true: shrinking stages, nested part-of-whole sizes, or quantitative transfer; false: a pie split, a process flowchart, or a hierarchy without quantities.
